Could you please tell me what the acronym ETL stands for? It came up in a job description I am reviewing.
--- reference
ETL stands for Extract, Transform, Load. It describes the process of pulling data from source systems, reshaping or cleaning it, and then loading it into a destination such as a data warehouse. In a job description it usually points to a data engineering or analytics role.

--- reference

=== J-0025
--- user
quick q, what does ETL stand for? seeing it in a data team doc
--- reference
ETL means Extract, Transform, Load. Data is pulled out of its sources, cleaned and reshaped, then loaded into a target system like a warehouse. The related ELT variant just flips the last two steps and transforms the data after loading.
--- derived

=== J-0023
--- user
Safe to retry a GET?
--- reference
Yes. GET is meant to be safe and idempotent, so repeating it does not change anything on the server. The exception is an endpoint that wrongly triggers side effects on GET, so it is worth checking that one is not misbehaving.
--- derived

=== J-0041
--- user
what's a DNS TTL
--- reference
A DNS TTL, or time to live, is how long a resolver may cache a DNS record before asking again. A short TTL makes changes spread quickly but adds lookups, while a long one reduces traffic but delays updates.
--- derived

=== J-0004
--- user
Could you explain what cache invalidation means? I would like a clear definition for our team notes.
--- reference
Cache invalidation is the process of removing or refreshing cached data once the original data has changed, so users do not keep seeing stale content. It is notoriously hard because you must decide when and how much to evict without throwing away the performance benefit of the cache.
--- derived
